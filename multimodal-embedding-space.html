Multimodal Embedding Space:
How EmbeddingGemma 2 Puts Five Modalities in One Vector

A 740M model maps text, code, images, video, and audio into the same 768-dimensional space. Modularity means you ship only the encoders you need; Matryoshka truncation means the same comparison works at 128d, 256d, 512d, or 768d.

  • What "natively multimodal" embedding space actually means under the hood
  • The modular encoder trick: 270M / 440M / 570M / 740M on demand, same vector
  • How Matryoshka truncation turns one model into a cheap first-stage retriever
  • Why task instructions are a first-class control, not a hack
THE MODEL IN ONE SENTENCE

Each input modality enters through a small specialized encoder, but every encoder projects into the same 768-dimensional space, so a text query can be cosine-compared directly against an audio clip, a video frame, or a screenshot of code.

THE MENTAL MODEL

Four encoders, one shared vector

Most embedding systems bolt on a separate text encoder, a separate image encoder, a separate audio encoder, and a hand-written bridge that fakes alignment. EmbeddingGemma 2 takes a different bet: the encoders exist, but they were trained to agree on a single coordinate system. Once you accept that, every comparison you can do with text alone (nearest neighbors, MMR, shortlists) just works across modalities, with no glue.

FIGURE 1 · Modality encoders feed projections into one shared 768-dimensional vector space.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Once you have a shared space, every operation you already trust on text embeddings works on audio and image embeddings for free. The bridge problem is gone. Your reranker, your MMR, your shortlist, your ANN index: all the same code, all the same params, all the same recall curve.

WALKTHROUGH · PART 1

Cross-modal retrieval is the problem this solves

The classic embedding pipeline assumes a single modality. You embed a query, you embed your corpus, you return the nearest neighbors. That works until the user wants to "find the video clip that matches this audio", or "show me the screenshot of code that explains this paragraph", or "what did the customer say in the call when they talked about this UI". Cross-modal retrieval means the query and the candidate can be in different modalities, and a system that only handles text forces you to transcribe, OCR, or caption first. That round trip is where the latency, the privacy leak, and the recall drop happen.

FIGURE 2 · The transcription step is gone. The round trip to a remote server is gone.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Edge inference changes the cost model. Once the embedder runs on the device, the cost of indexing a five-minute meeting or an hour of security footage is the battery cost of running the model. That is qualitatively different from a per-call API bill. For personal-scale search, the right answer is "embed everything you have, never leave the device".

WALKTHROUGH · PART 2

Modular encoders: 270M, 440M, 570M, or 740M on demand

The architectural bet EmbeddingGemma 2 makes is that modality encoders are additive, not substitutive. The text-and-code encoder is the foundation: 270M parameters, an 8K context window, based on the Gemma 4 decoder. On top of that, you can load a vision encoder (+170M, handles images, video frames, and document screenshots) and an audio encoder (+300M, raw 16kHz mono audio, no transcription). Any subset is a valid model. That is the whole trick: a developer who only does text RAG ships 270M; a developer doing code-and-screenshot search ships 440M; a developer doing voice memo search ships 570M; only the full multimodal consumer pays the 740M cost.

FIGURE 3 · Every configuration projects into the same 768-d space; the encoder mix is the only thing that changes.

WHY THIS MATTERS

The pattern is "load the encoders you actually use, never the others." For a mobile app that only does text RAG, the 270M text-only model fits in 191MB of RAM. For a desktop tool that indexes screen recordings, the same loader pulls the audio encoder at runtime, the rest of the pipeline is identical. The system is the same code; the deployment target decides which encoders ship.

WALKTHROUGH · PART 3

Matryoshka truncation: same vector, four resolutions

Matryoshka Representation Learning, the Kusupati et al. 2022 paper that this model uses, is the trick that turns one trained model into four storage tiers. The idea is simple to state: during training, the model is forced to put the most important information in the first 128 dimensions, the next layer in the next 128, and so on. At inference time you can take the first 128, 256, 512, or 768 dimensions of the vector and the comparisons still work, with a known quality dropoff. For text and code you keep 90% of the quality at 128d; for images, video, and audio you keep about 75%. Both are good enough for a first-stage shortlist, where you have a reranker downstream.

FIGURE 4 · The shorter vectors are prefixes of the full one. Quality degrades gracefully, not catastrophically.

WHY THIS MATTERS

This is the part that makes the system actually deployable. A shortlist at 128d is six times cheaper to store and much faster to score than the full 768d. You embed your whole corpus at 768d, project to 128d for the ANN index, and only expand the top-K to 768d before reranking. The full precision vector is never moved across the disk for the 99% of candidates that get filtered out.

WALKTHROUGH · PART 4

Task instructions reshape the vector at inference time

One thing that surprises first-time users of EmbeddingGemma 2 is that the same input, run with different prompt_name values, produces meaningfully different vectors. SearchQuery encodes "I am a question, find the document that answers me." Document encodes "I am a passage, here is what I am about." A symmetric pair, trained jointly, pushes relevant (query, document) pairs together and irrelevant pairs apart. This is the same pattern that was popular in the dense retrieval literature years ago, but the difference is that here the instruction vocabulary is built into the model and you do not need to fine-tune to add a new task.

FIGURE 5 · Query and document prompts are trained to make relevant pairs cluster and irrelevant ones repel.

WHY THIS MATTERS

For retrieval, mixing SearchQuery and Document prompts is the difference between a useful system and a useless one. The model knows that queries and documents live in different halves of the same space, and it has spent training time pushing them apart when they are unrelated and pulling them together when they are related. The right failure mode to remember: if your recall drops, check that you are not accidentally encoding the document with the query prompt.

WALKTHROUGH · PART 5

The 8K context window is shared across modalities

Every modality feeds the same 8,192-token context. Audio at 16 kHz mono gets roughly 5.5 minutes per call; images get up to 29; video frames up to 58 at one frame per second. The relevant mental model is "tokens of attention" rather than wall-clock time. A long voice memo and a long document cost the same in tokens, and the model decides on its own how to spend them. The interesting consequence is that you can interleave: a product listing with a photo, a short video, and a description becomes one embedding, not three.

FIGURE 6 · Modality-specific rates decide how the shared 8K token budget gets spent.

WHY THIS MATTERS

For RAG, the context window determines how much you can pre-pend to a query. For multimodal retrieval, it is the unit of "how rich can one embedding be". A single 8K embedding that holds text, an image, and a 30-second video clip is qualitatively different from three separate 768-d vectors you have to fuse yourself. The model has done the fusion during encoding; the index stores the result.

COMMON CONFUSIONS

Four things people get wrong the first time

01 "Truncating the vector means throwing away information, so it is always worse."

A Matryoshka-trained vector is not a generic vector that got chopped. The first 128 dims were chosen during training to carry the bulk of the signal; the remaining 640 dims are residual refinement. For text and code you keep about 90% of the quality at 128d, and for image, video, and audio you keep about 75%. That is the same recall curve a 6x larger random vector would give you, and it is much better than the obvious comparison.

02 "Four encoders mean four embedding spaces, so I still have to bridge them at query time."

No. The encoders are trained to project into the same 768-d space, so a text vector, an image vector, and an audio vector are directly comparable. There is no projection matrix at query time, no shared anchor embedding, no contrastive fine-tune on your data. The model was trained to align them, and the alignment is the whole product. You compute cosine similarity and that is it.

03 "Bigger is always better, so I should ship the 740M model even for text RAG."

For text-only workloads, the 270M configuration is best-in-class for its size and matches or beats much larger models on MTEB. The 170M vision encoder and 300M audio encoder are not free; they cost RAM, latency, and battery. The right question is which modalities you actually need at query time, not which modalities you might want someday. If you only do text, ship 270M.

04 "Task instructions are a hack for prompt engineering."

The model was trained with a vocabulary of task instructions baked in, and switching prompt_name between SearchQuery and Document produces two different vectors that the model has been explicitly optimized to compare. This is a trained behavior, not a prompt-engineering trick. The right mental model: the same encoder, conditioned on a task, lives in a different region of the same space, and the training objective made sure that the regions line up where they should.
